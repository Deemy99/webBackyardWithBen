# Off-Page SEO Plan: Backyard with Ben

**Site:** https://www.backyardwithben.com
**Who this is for:** the site owner. No technical knowledge needed.
**Time budget:** about 3-4 hours a week, done by one person.

## What this plan is (and isn't)

"Off-page SEO" means getting other websites and communities to mention and link to Backyard with Ben. Google treats those links as votes of trust. This is a **manual plan**: you do every step yourself, one at a time.

**Things we never do** (each one risks a Google penalty or a damaged reputation):

- No automated outreach tools, mass emails, or bots.
- No comment spam, and no dropping links in forums where they weren't asked for.
- No buying links, link exchanges, private blog networks (PBNs), or link "packages".
- No mass guest posting and no paid or "sponsored" guest posts.
- No fake profiles, fake reviews, or sockpuppet accounts.
- No made-up statistics, and no claims that we tested products. Nobody physically tests the products we recommend, so we never say "we tested" or "hands-on".

**Honesty about Emma:** Emma, the host, is AI-generated. When you pitch, answer journalist questions, or write a bio, the answers come from **"the Backyard with Ben team."** Never present Emma as a real person or a real expert. Where it matters (bios, expert-quote answers), say plainly: "Our host Emma is an AI-generated character."

## 1. Linkable assets

A "linkable asset" is a page people want to link to because it's useful, like a checklist, chart, or calendar. A plain article rarely earns links on its own. A printable resource does much better.

**Implemented now:** a printable Beginner Backyard Tool Checklist at /checklists/beginner-tool-checklist and a Lawn Care Calendar at /checklists/lawn-care-calendar.

| Asset | Builds on existing guide(s) | Who would link to it |
|---|---|---|
| **Beginner Backyard Tool Checklist** (live) | easy-diy-backyard-upgrades-weekend, raised-garden-bed, build-backyard-fire-pit, /tools | New-homeowner blogs, DIY resource pages |
| **Lawn Care Calendar** (live) | best-time-to-fertilize-lawn, how-to-overseed-a-lawn, get-rid-of-weeds-in-lawn, lawn-care-mistakes | Lawn and garden resource pages, homeowner newsletters |
| **Printable Raised-Bed Cut List** (planned) | raised-garden-bed, best-vegetables-raised-garden-beds-beginners | School/community garden sites, beginner gardening blogs |
| **"When to Plant" Chart** (planned) | when-to-plant-a-garden, how-to-prepare-garden-soil | Community gardens, library and extension-style resource lists |
| **Grill Cleaning Checklist** (optional) | how-to-clean-a-grill, grilling-mistakes-ruin-food | BBQ blogs, grilling forums (when someone asks) |
| **Steak Temperature & Timing Card** (optional) | grill-perfect-steak-temperature-timing, grilling-tips | BBQ and cooking resource lists |
| **Privacy Planting Planner** (optional) | best-privacy-trees-for-backyard, backyard-privacy-without-fence | Landscaping and homeowner blogs |

Rules for every asset:

- Keep affiliate links on the asset page to a minimum. People link to useful pages, not shopping pages.
- Planting dates depend on location, so any planting or lawn timing should point readers to their local frost dates or their local extension service. Don't promise exact dates.
- Each one gets its own Pinterest pin (see Section 2).

## 2. Backlink sources and how to approach each

### Pinterest (start here, biggest fit for this niche)
- **Set up:** create a Pinterest **business** account and **claim the website** (Pinterest walks you through it). Once the site is claimed, Pinterest can show rich pins, which pull the title and description straight from the page.
- **Boards:** about 6-8 that match the site's categories: Raised Beds & Vegetable Gardening, Lawn Care, Low-Maintenance Plants & Trees, Backyard Privacy, Patio & Outdoor Living, Grilling & BBQ, Weekend DIY Projects, Printables & Checklists.
- **Pins per guide:** aim for 2-3 different vertical pin images per guide (different title or photo). That's roughly 70-100 pins for the 34 guides, spread over the 90 days, not all at once.
- **Pace:** a few pins a day, scheduled with Pinterest's own built-in scheduler. Scheduling your own pins is fine. Don't use tools that mass-follow or mass-repin.
- Pinterest links are "nofollow", so they don't pass ranking power directly. They do bring visitors, and that's how other bloggers find you and later link to you.
- When a pin links to /shop or /tools, add a short affiliate disclosure in the description.

### Reddit and Facebook groups (genuine participation only)
- **Where:** gardening, lawn care, DIY, homeowner and grilling communities. Read each community's rules first. Many ban self-promotion completely.
- **Common rule of thumb (9:1):** for every 1 post that mentions your own site, make about 9 helpful contributions that don't. Where a community has its own rule, that rule wins.
- **How:** answer the question fully in the post itself. Only link a guide if it truly adds something, and say it's your site ("I wrote a guide on this for our site, Backyard with Ben"). Never post affiliate links there.
- Use one honest personal account, run by you. Don't post as "Emma."
- The value here is visitors and brand awareness. These links rarely help rankings directly.

### Expert-quote platforms
- Services like **Qwoted, Featured.com, Help a B2B Writer and Source of Sources** send you journalists' and bloggers' questions so you can answer them. These platforms change often (some go paid, some shut down or relaunch), so check what's active and free when you start.
- **Approach:** answer only questions in our lane (lawn, garden, raised beds, grilling, patios, DIY). Keep it short and specific, and send it the same day if you can.
- **Honesty:** sign answers as "the Backyard with Ben team." Don't claim personal credentials, years of experience, or product testing. If a request asks for a credentialed expert (a certified arborist or landscape architect, for example), skip it.
- Expect most answers not to get used. A few placements over 90 days is a good result.

### Guest posts (few, high quality)
- **Target:** at most 1-2 in 90 days, on real gardening, DIY or homeowner blogs that have their own audience and a "write for us" or contributor page.
- **Avoid:** sites that charge, sites full of unrelated "write for us" posts, and anything that looks like a link farm.
- **Content:** a genuinely new piece written for their readers, not a copy of our guide. Accept whatever link and author bio the editor allows, and never pay. The bio should state that Emma is an AI-generated host, or simply credit "the Backyard with Ben team."

### Resource-page outreach
Many sites keep "useful links" pages. Find them in Google with searches like these:
- `"gardening resources" inurl:links`
- `"lawn care" "helpful links"`
- `"vegetable gardening" "resources" inurl:resources`
- `"community garden" "resources"`
- `"new homeowner" "resources" checklist`

Pitch the **asset** that fits the page (checklist, calendar, chart), not a product page. Only contact pages that are clearly kept up to date, and send one email per site. A single follow-up is fine; after that, stop.

### Unlinked brand mentions
- Set up a free **Google Alert** for "Backyard with Ben" and "backyardwithben".
- When a site mentions us without linking, send one polite note (template below).

### Our own social profiles
- **Pinterest:** the priority, covered above.
- **Facebook page:** already exists. Share each new guide and each asset there.
- **Instagram:** optional. Reuse the pin images and post 2-3 times a week.
- **YouTube Shorts:** optional. Only do it if it stays honest: no implying a real person is testing products, and label AI-generated host content clearly.
- Put the same name, logo and website link on every profile. Consistency helps Google connect them to the site.

## 3. Outreach templates (short and honest)

Personalize the first line every time. Never send these in bulk.

**A. Resource page**
> Subject: A free resource for your [page name] page
>
> Hi [Name], I came across your [page name] page while looking for [topic] resources. It's a great list. We just published a free printable [Lawn Care Calendar / Beginner Backyard Tool Checklist]: [URL]. No sign-up needed. If you think your readers would find it useful, we'd be glad to be included. Either way, thanks for keeping the list up.
> — [Your name], Backyard with Ben

**B. Guest post pitch**
> Subject: Article idea for [Site]: [working title]
>
> Hi [Name], I read your recent post on [topic] and liked [specific point]. I'd like to pitch an original article for your readers: "[Title]", covering [3 short bullet points]. It hasn't been published anywhere else. A note on us: Backyard with Ben is a backyard and gardening guide site. Our host, Emma, is an AI-generated character, and our content is written and checked by our team. Happy to follow your guidelines and accept your edits.
> — [Your name]

**C. Unlinked mention**
> Subject: Thanks for mentioning Backyard with Ben
>
> Hi [Name], thanks for mentioning Backyard with Ben in [article title]. We appreciate it. If it's easy, a link to [specific URL] would help readers find the guide you mentioned. No worries if not.
> — [Your name]

**D. Expert-quote answer**
> Hi [Journalist], here's a response from the Backyard with Ben team (backyardwithben.com). Note: our site host, Emma, is an AI-generated character, so please attribute this to "the Backyard with Ben team," not to an individual expert.
>
> [2-4 sentence direct answer to the exact question. Practical advice only. No statistics unless they come from a named, linkable source.]
>
> Source link if useful: [relevant guide URL]

## 4. 90-day schedule (about 3-4 hours a week)

Targets are goals, not guarantees. Links take time, and a "no" or no reply is normal.

| Week | Tasks | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Create Pinterest business account, claim the website, make 6-8 boards. Set up Google Alerts. Make one tracking spreadsheet (date, site, contact, status). Set up Search Console, Bing and Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (Section 5). | Accounts live; baseline numbers recorded |
| **2** | Pins for both live checklists, plus 5 guides (2 pins each). Join 3-4 relevant Reddit/Facebook communities, read their rules, start helping (no links). Sign up for 1-2 expert-quote platforms. | ~12 pins; 5 helpful comments |
| **3** | Pins for 5 more guides. Answer 2-3 relevant expert-quote requests. Keep helping in communities. | ~10 pins; 2-3 quote answers |
| **4** | Resource-page research: find 15 good pages using the search operators. Send 5 resource-page emails. | 5 emails sent |
| **5** | Pins for 5 guides. Send 5 more resource-page emails. Quote answers as they come up. | ~10 pins; 5 emails |
| **6** | Shortlist 5 guest-post target blogs and read their guidelines. Send 1-2 pitches. Follow up once on week-4 emails. | 1-2 pitches |
| **7** | Pins for 5 guides. Community participation (first self-link only if you've made ~9 helpful posts and the rules allow it). Check Google Alerts. | ~10 pins; 1 linked post max |
| **8** | Monthly check (Section 5). Send 5 resource-page emails, this time pitching the next asset if it's ready. | 5 emails; monthly log updated |
| **9** | Build the Printable Raised-Bed Cut List or "When to Plant" chart (with your developer if needed). Pin it. | 1 new asset |
| **10** | Write the guest post if a pitch was accepted. Otherwise pitch 1-2 more blogs. Quote answers. | 1 draft or 1-2 pitches |
| **11** | Pins for the remaining guides, plus a second pin image for the best-performing guides. Send 5 resource-page emails featuring the new asset. | ~10 pins; 5 emails |
| **12** | Follow up once on outstanding emails. Send unlinked-mention notes if any came up. Community participation. | Follow-ups done |
| **13** | 90-day review: what got links or traffic, what didn't. Drop the weakest channel and double down on the best. Plan the next 90 days. | Review written |

**Realistic 90-day totals:** about 70-100 pins, 25-30 personal outreach emails, 1-2 guest pitches, a steady trickle of quote answers, and 1 new asset. A handful of real editorial links from relevant sites would be a solid start for a new site.

## 5. Monitoring

### Tools (all free)
- **Google Search Console, Links report:** shows which sites link to you and which pages they link to. Google's data updates slowly, so new links can take weeks to appear.
- **Bing Webmaster Tools, Backlinks:** a second view of your links. You can import the site straight from Search Console.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools (free for verified site owners):** backlink list, referring domains, and alerts for new and lost links.

### What to track monthly (one row per month in the spreadsheet)
1. **Referring domains:** how many different websites link to you. This matters more than the total number of links.
2. **New links gained and links lost**, with the site name and which page each one points to.
3. **Which pages earn links:** expect the checklists and calendar to lead. If they don't, rethink the pitch.
4. **Outreach results:** emails sent, replies, links won.
5. **Pinterest:** outbound clicks to the site, and your top 5 pins.
6. **Search Console Performance:** total clicks and impressions, plus the top pages, so you can see whether links are lifting rankings.
7. **Red flags:** sudden batches of links from spammy or unrelated sites. You didn't build these and usually don't need to act. Google generally ignores them. Only discuss a disavow file with an SEO professional if there's a manual action notice in Search Console.

**Be patient:** links usually take months to affect rankings. Judge this plan after 90 days, not after two weeks.
